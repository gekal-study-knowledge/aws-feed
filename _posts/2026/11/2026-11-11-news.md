<p>MySQL 26.10 includes bug fixes, security patches, and some improvements to READ COMMITTED workload performance and purge lag warnings in the error log. On busy READ COMMITTED workloads, NUMA systems performance could be impacted as each read view that closed required a global mutex. Read-view objects can now be reused which eliminates the global mutex on closure. The error log now reports warnings when the history-list length (HLL) crosses innodb_max_purge_lag and when purge lag begins delaying DML, so you can detect purge backlogs before they affect performance. To learn more, please refer to the <a href="https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/26.10/en/mysql-nutshell.html" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">MySQL 26.10 release notes</a>.</p> 
<p>Amazon RDS Database Preview Environment database instances are retained for a maximum period of 60 days and are automatically deleted after the retention period. Amazon RDS Database Preview Environment database instances are priced as per the <a href="https://aws.amazon.com/rds/mysql/pricing/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">pricing</a> in the US East (Ohio) Region.</p>

### Amazon OpenSearch Service announces managed cross-Region disaster recovery

- **Link**: [https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/amazon-opensearch-service-managed-cross-region-disaster-recovery/](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/amazon-opensearch-service-managed-cross-region-disaster-recovery/)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 04:00:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-11 11:29:35

<details class="entry-summary">
<summary>詳細を表示</summary>
<p>Amazon OpenSearch Service now offers managed cross-Region disaster recovery, so you can meet strict availability and business continuity requirements for mission-critical search, AI, and analytics workloads without building or maintaining replication yourself. After you create domains in two Regions, you can pair them with a single API call or a few steps in the AWS Management Console.</p> 
<p>Managed disaster recovery delivers a recovery point objective (RPO) of under one minute and a recovery time objective (RTO) of under 20 minutes. The secondary domain stays continuously in sync with the primary. For planned events such as maintenance or Region rotation, a switchover swaps the two domains' roles with zero data loss. If your primary domain becomes unavailable, a failover promotes the secondary, with data loss limited to the replication lag. After a failover, you restore protection by pairing the new primary with a secondary domain. You can monitor replication health and lag with Amazon CloudWatch metrics.</p> 
<p>Managed disaster recovery requires OpenSearch 3.7 or later and supports domains in the same AWS account, including domains encrypted with customer managed AWS Key Management Service (AWS KMS) keys. Domains that use UltraWarm storage or OpenSearch Optimized instances (OR1 or OR2) aren't supported. If you're on an earlier version, <a href="https://docs.aws.amazon.com/opensearch-service/latest/developerguide/version-migration.html">clone-based upgrades</a> let you test OpenSearch 3.7 against your workload, cut over when you're ready, and roll back if needed.</p> 
<p>There's no additional charge for managed disaster recovery. You pay standard rates for the secondary domain and cross-Region data transfer. For details, see <a href="https://aws.amazon.com/opensearch-service/pricing/">Amazon OpenSearch Service pricing</a>.</p> 
<p>Managed disaster recovery is available today in all commercial AWS Regions where Amazon OpenSearch Service is available (<a href="https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/opensearch-service.html">Region list</a>). To get started, see the <a href="https://docs.aws.amazon.com/opensearch-service/latest/developerguide/disaster-recovery.html">disaster recovery documentation</a>.</p>
</details>

### AWS Billing and Cost Management now supports AWS Billing Conductor and AWS Billing Transfer views as source views for custom billing views

- **Link**: [https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/aws-billing-and-cost-management-multi-source-custom-billing-views](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/aws-billing-and-cost-management-multi-source-custom-billing-views)
- **Published**: 2026-11-11 08:22:00
- **Fetched**: 2026-11-11 11:29:35

<div class="entry-summary">
<p>Custom billing views now support AWS Billing Conductor and AWS Billing Transfer views as source views. These are the views AWS maintains per billing group and per billing transfer relationship. You can combine them into a single custom billing view, apply filters, and use that view in Cost Explorer, AWS Budgets, and the Billing and Cost Management console. There are three source view types. Billing group views contain the cost of each Billing Conductor billing group, which you can combine for chargeback and showback across business units. Billing transfer views contain the cost transferred to you for each end customer, and transfer showback views contain the cost that end customer sees. Aggregating each type gives you your total transferred cost and the total bill your end customers see.</p> 
<p>Your access does not change: you can use an AWS-managed view as a source only where you can already access it. Once created, you can share a custom billing view using AWS Resource Access Manager with accounts inside or outside your organization, so your finance team or a downstream seller works with the data in their own account. Whether you are an AWS Partner reselling AWS services or an organization using billing groups for pro forma billing, you get one consolidated view instead of one per customer or group.</p> 
<p>This feature is available in all commercial AWS Regions at no additional cost. To get started, navigate to Billing Views in the AWS Billing and Cost Management console or view the <a href="https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cost-management/latest/userguide/billing-view.html">user guide</a>.</p>
</div>
